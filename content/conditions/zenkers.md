## What it is

Zenker's diverticulum is a pouch at the very top of the esophagus. When the cricopharyngeus does not open well, every swallow builds up pressure just above it. Over time that pressure pushes the lining of the throat out through a weak spot in the muscle wall, called Killian's triangle, and makes a pocket that food can fall into [@statpearlsZenker; @esge2020]. Laryngopedia describes it as a hernia above a sphincter that will not relax, which makes A-CPD the problem underneath [@laryngopediaZenker].

It is mostly found in people in their seventies and eighties, more often men, and it is rare before 40 [@esge2020; @statpearlsZenker]. Roughly 2 people per 100,000 are diagnosed each year [@krekeler2024].

## What it feels like

Food sticks high in the throat. Most people with a Zenker's pouch, around 80 to 90 percent, have trouble swallowing [@dellanna2024]. In a study of 165 patients, 89% had trouble swallowing, 66% had a cough and 59% had food coming back up [@martinezparedes2022].

What comes back up is often undigested, sometimes well after the meal, because it has been sitting in the pouch. Breath can smell bad no matter how much you brush. There may be gurgling in the neck when you swallow [@dellanna2024; @ramamurthy2025]. Some people get a hoarse voice or lose weight [@statpearlsZenker].

The risk to take seriously is food from the pouch going into the lungs, which can cause pneumonia [@dellanna2024].

## What tells it apart from the ones next to it

**From A-CPD.** The same tight muscle sits underneath both. The pouch is what adds the undigested food coming back up, the bad breath and the gurgling in the neck [@dellanna2024; @ramamurthy2025; @laryngopediaZenker].

**From achalasia.** Achalasia is at the bottom of the esophagus, affects drinks as well as food, is usually diagnosed between 25 and 60, and is confirmed with manometry [@farrokhi2007]. Zenker's is at the top, is mostly found after 70, and is diagnosed with a barium swallow [@esge2020]. Both can bring food back up undigested, which is one reason they get compared.

**From R-CPD.** R-CPD is about air that cannot get out, starts in childhood and does not make food stick [@laryngopediaRcpd]. Gurgling is the overlap. In R-CPD it comes after eating or drinking and goes with bloating and no burp. In Zenker's it comes from the neck as you swallow [@bastian2019; @ramamurthy2025].

## How it gets confirmed

The recommended test is a barium swallow recorded on video X-ray, called videofluoroscopy [@esge2020]. An endoscopy helps rule out other causes and plan treatment. Manometry is not routinely needed [@esge2020; @statpearlsZenker].

The people to ask for are an ear, nose and throat doctor or a gastroenterologist, and the test to ask about by name is a barium swallow.

## How it gets treated

A small pouch that is not causing symptoms does not need treatment [@statpearlsZenker]. When it does, every treatment cuts the cricopharyngeus, so that the pouch and the esophagus become one channel and food stops collecting [@dellanna2024; @esge2020].

- **Open surgery.** A cut in the side of the neck. The pouch is removed or tacked up out of the way, and the muscle is cut [@esge2020].
- **Rigid endoscopy.** Through the mouth under general anesthesia, using a stapler or a CO2 laser to divide the wall between the pouch and the esophagus. It needs enough neck movement to get the scope in [@ramamurthy2025].
- **Flexible endoscopy (septotomy).** A flexible scope cuts the same wall, often without general anesthesia [@esge2020].
- **Z-POEM.** A tunnel is made under the lining, the muscle is cut inside the tunnel, and the entrance is closed with clips [@ramamurthy2025]. The 2020 European guideline still called this experimental [@esge2020]. Studies published since then report good results [@zhang2022].

## What the evidence says

- **Z-POEM.** Across 11 studies with 357 patients, 93% did well (95% CI 89 to 95). Symptoms came back in 11% (95% CI 8 to 16) and complications happened in 12% [@zhang2022].
- **Flexible, rigid and open treatment do similarly.** Across 8 studies with 1,281 patients there was no clear difference in success, and flexible endoscopy meant about two fewer days in hospital [@cadena2023].
- **Open surgery may last better than laser.** In a network analysis of 903 patients, open surgery had lower odds of symptoms staying or coming back than laser treatment (odds ratio 0.20, 95% CI 0.04 to 0.91) [@bhatt2021].
- **Most people get much better.** In a prospective study of 147 patients, 66% reported complete improvement on a standard swallowing questionnaire [@howell2024].
- **How sure we can be.** Almost all of this comes from observational studies, many looking back at old records. There are no large randomized trials, so comparisons between treatments are uncertain.
